Statistics and cybersecurity

This blog is my course journal for Statistics during the 2026/27 academic year. I use it to organize homework, revisit theoretical ideas, and connect statistical methods with computational practice. I am interested in how careful data analysis can help make complex technical systems easier to understand: how to summarize observations without hiding important variation, how to distinguish a meaningful signal from random noise, and how to communicate uncertainty rather than presenting estimates as certainties.

Some topics I would like to explore further include the statistical patterns found in security events, ways to evaluate anomaly-detection methods, and how sampling choices affect conclusions drawn from network or system measurements. These are areas of curiosity rather than claims about completed research. They raise practical questions: how representative is a dataset, what does a model miss, how often might an alert be a false positive, and how can an analysis be reproduced and checked by someone else?

How I approach learning

I value explanations that connect equations to assumptions, code, and interpretation. A result is more useful when its limitations are visible: the data source is understood, the method is appropriate to the question, and the conclusions stay within what the evidence supports. Through this blog, I aim to make my learning process traceable by showing worked examples, computational steps, visualizations, and reflections alongside the final answers.
